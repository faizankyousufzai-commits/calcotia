  1. Enter your Quantitative Reasoning section score, from 60 to 90.

Why Use This Tool

  • Built for the current test. The GMAT Focus Edition has three sections, each scored 60 to 90, and a total from 205 to 805.
  • Equal weight, done right. All three sections count equally, so a weak section pulls the total down as much as any other.
  • Honest about estimates. GMAC does not publish its exact conversion, so this total is an estimate from the widely used section-sum formula.
  • Instant comparison. Try different section scores to see what you need for your target total.
  • Free and simple. No sign-up, no app. Enter three numbers and get your total.

Who Can Use It

  • MBA applicants. Check what total your practice section scores point to before test day.
  • Retakers. See how many more section points you need for your target score.
  • Tutors and coaches. Show students how each section contributes to the total.
  • GRE takers comparing tests. If you took the GRE, use the concordance note below to compare it with a GMAT total.

Tips for Best Results

  • Enter scores from the same test. Mixing sections from different sittings misleads.
  • Work on your weakest section first. The lowest score is the cheapest place to gain points.
  • Every section point is worth about 6 to 7 total points, so small gains add up.
  • Use official practice exams for your baseline. Third-party section scores can run a little high or low.
  • Treat the estimate as a range. If this tool says 655, your official total could be 645 to 665.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is GMAT score calculated?

Your total comes from your three section scores, each from 60 to 90: Quantitative Reasoning, Verbal Reasoning, and Data Insights. GMAC combines them with equal weight.

How is GMAT total score calculated from section scores?

Add the three section scores together, a sum from 180 to 270. This calculator maps that sum onto the 205 to 805 scale, rounded to the nearest score ending in 5.

How is the GMAT score calculated differently from the old GMAT?

The old GMAT ran from 200 to 800, ending in 0, and used only Quant and Verbal. The Focus Edition runs from 205 to 805, ending in 5, and counts all three sections.

What is a good GMAT Focus score?

A total of 645 beats about 86% of test takers, and 705 beats about 98%. Scores of 705 and above suit the most selective MBA programs.

Can I compare a GRE score with a GMAT score?

Yes. A GRE to GMAT score calculator comparison uses GMAC's official concordance table, which lines up scores by percentile. A GRE total near the 86th percentile lines up with a GMAT Focus total near 645.
