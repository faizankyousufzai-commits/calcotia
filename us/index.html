What the United States calculators cover

The United States calculators cover everyday questions that are easy to get wrong by hand: how much gravel, topsoil, sod, brick or wallpaper a project needs, when a pregnant dog is due and what to expect each week, how big a puppy will grow, the final-exam score needed to reach a target grade, AP, SAT, ACT, LSAT and GMAT scores, the eye colors a baby is likely to inherit, how much retirement savings can safely be withdrawn each year, how big an emergency fund to keep, HSA growth and 2026 limits, wedding budgets by guest count, coffee ratios, jet lag recovery, nap length, solar panels for a home, and daily water intake. Each calculator asks only for the few details it needs and shows the result right away, with the working explained in plain words.

Every result is an estimate, not professional advice. A dog's due date can shift by a few days and a puppy's adult weight depends on breed, diet and health, so check anything important with your vet. Test-score results are estimates too, because official scoring uses equating and adaptive methods, so confirm any target with your teacher or the test maker. The 4 percent rule and the HSA figures are planning guides, not promises about markets or taxes, so talk to a financial planner before making retirement decisions. Material quantities are planning estimates as well: densities and waste vary by supplier, so confirm the order with your supplier before you buy.
