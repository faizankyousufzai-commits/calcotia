Who Can Use It

  • Workers with a high-deductible health plan. You can only contribute with a qualifying HDHP, and not enrolled in Medicare.
  • Anyone deciding how much to contribute. Use this page as your how much should i contribute to my hsa calculator: start with the employer match, then raise it toward the limit.
  • People aged 55 or older. The extra $1,000 catch-up makes the last working years the best time to save in an HSA.

Tips for Best Results

  • Take the full employer contribution first. Never leave employer money on the table: it counts toward the limit.
  • Learn how to calculate HSA contribution room. Subtract your employer contribution from the IRS limit.
  • Invest once your balance covers your deductible. Many providers let you invest above a $1,000 cash threshold.
  • Keep receipts for medical bills. Pay now or reimburse yourself years later, if you keep the receipts.
  • Fix excess contributions fast. Remove the excess plus earnings before the tax deadline to avoid the 6% excise tax.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an HSA?

A tax-advantaged account for people with a high-deductible health plan. Money goes in, grows, and comes out tax-free for qualified medical expenses. It stays with you if you change jobs.

What are the 2026 HSA contribution limits?

For 2026 the IRS limit is $4,400 self-only and $8,750 family, under Revenue Procedure 2025-19. People 55 or older can add a $1,000 catch-up. A qualifying HDHP needs a $1,700 self-only or $3,400 family deductible.

How much should I contribute to my HSA?

At least enough to get any employer contribution, then as much as your budget allows up to the limit.

How to calculate earnings on excess HSA contributions?

Excess contributions face a 6% excise tax each year they stay in the account. Withdraw the excess plus earnings before the tax deadline. Your provider works out the earnings: that is how do you calculate earnings on excess HSA contributions in practice.

Can I invest my HSA money?

Yes, most providers offer funds once your balance passes $1,000. Invested HSA money grows tax-free, so many treat the HSA as a retirement account too.
