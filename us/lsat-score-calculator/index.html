How to Use Calcotia's LSAT Score Calculator

  1. Enter your correct answers for Logical Reasoning 1, out of about 25 questions.
  2. Enter your correct answers for Logical Reasoning 2, out of about 25 questions.
  3. Enter your correct answers for Reading Comprehension, out of about 27 questions.
  4. Click Calculate.
  5. Read your raw score and your estimated scaled score. That is how to calculate LSAT score estimates from a practice test.

Why Use This Tool

  • Built for the current test. Since August 2024 the LSAT has two Logical Reasoning sections and one Reading Comprehension section.
  • Raw to scaled in one step. Enter correct answers per section; it totals them and converts the raw score.
  • Honest about estimates. LSAC converts each test with its own table, so this result can differ by a point or two.
  • No penalty for guessing. Wrong answers cost nothing, so your raw score is simply the number you got right.
  • Free and instant. Run as many practice tests through it as you like, with no sign-up.

Who Can Use It

  • LSAT students. Check your practice test score right after you finish, without waiting for a prep company's report.
  • Tutors. Show students how each extra correct answer moves their scaled score.
  • Retakers. Compare practice tests over time to see real progress.
  • Applicants planning ahead. See what raw score you need for your target school's median.

Tips for Best Results

  • Count only the three scored sections. The unscored experimental section does not count.
  • Always answer every question. There is no deduction for wrong answers, so a guess can only help.
  • Logical Reasoning is about two-thirds of your score. Extra work there pays off most.
  • Use recent practice tests. Older tests with a logic games section do not match the current format.
  • Treat the estimate as a range. If this tool says 165, your official score could be 164 to 166.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is LSAT score calculated?

Your raw score is the number you answer correctly across the three scored sections, about 75 to 78 questions. LSAC converts it to a scaled score from 120 to 180.

What raw score do I need for a 170?

You usually need about 68 to 69 correct out of about 77. For a 160, about 53 to 55 correct. For a 180, about 75 or more correct.

How is the LSAT score calculated on different test dates?

LSAC uses equating, which adjusts the table for each test date. A harder test needs a few fewer correct answers for the same score, and an easier test needs a few more.

How are LSAT scores calculated for percentiles?

Your percentile rank shows the share of test takers who scored below you over the previous three testing years. A 170 sits around the 95th percentile, a 160 around the 73rd, and a 150 around the 37th.

What is a good LSAT score?

The median is around 152 to 154. A 160 or above is strong for many law schools, and 170 or above is competitive for the most selective schools.
