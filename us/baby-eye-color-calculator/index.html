Our free baby eye color calculator predicts the likely eye colors for a baby based on both parents' eye colors. It's made for expecting parents, curious couples, and families who love a fun guess. Results show chances, not certainty, because eye color depends on many genes. Use it for fun, not as a medical test.

How the prediction works

This calculator uses a simplified Punnett-square model with three gene versions: B (brown), G (green) and b (blue), where brown beats green and green beats blue (B > G > b). Each parent's eye color stands for a mix of possible gene pairs: a brown-eyed parent is usually BB, but may carry green or blue; a green-eyed parent is usually GG, but may carry blue; a hazel-eyed parent carries brown and green, or green and blue; blue and gray eyes are always bb, and gray is grouped with blue. The baby gets one gene version from each parent, picked at random from that parent's pair. BB or Bb gives brown eyes, BG gives hazel, GG or Gb gives green, and bb gives blue. If a grandparent on a parent's side has blue or gray eyes, that parent is more likely to carry the blue gene, so the odds shift toward blue and green. For the grandparent step, Parent 1 is treated as the mother's side and Parent 2 as the father's side. Real eye color involves several genes, so these chances are estimates, not a certainty.

Tips for Best Results

  • Pick true colors. An eye color calculator can only work with what you enter. Check eyes in daylight.
  • Add grandparents when you can. A recessive trait can skip a generation.
  • Run the baby eye color calculator with and without grandparents to compare.
  • Remember newborn eyes change. Wait until around your baby's first birthday before judging the final color.
  • Share the result with family, but call it a guess. Eyes can surprise everyone.
  • Tell your pediatrician if an eye looks cloudy or the pupil looks white.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can two blue eyes make brown eyes?

It is rare, but yes. Simple charts say two blue-eyed parents have only blue-eyed children. Real eye color involves several genes, including OCA2 and HERC2, so a brown-eyed child is possible, though uncommon.

How accurate are eye color predictions?

They show odds, not certainty. Real eye color depends on many genes, so a baby can differ from the prediction. Use results for fun and planning, and expect your baby's true color to appear over the first year or so.

When do babies' eyes change color?

Some babies are born with blue or gray eyes. As melanin builds up, the color may deepen to green, hazel, or brown. It often settles around 9 to 12 months, though some babies keep changing for longer.

How does a Punnett square eye color calculator work?

A Punnett square pairs each parent's gene versions to show possible combinations. In the simple version, brown is dominant over blue. It is a handy teaching model, but real eye color is more complex, so results are estimates.
