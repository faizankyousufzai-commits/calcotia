How to Use the 4 Percent Rule Calculator

  1. Enter your total retirement savings.
  2. Keep the 4% withdrawal rate or adjust it.
  3. Click Calculate.
  4. Read your yearly and monthly withdrawal amounts.
  5. Enter a spending goal to see the nest egg you need.

Why Use This Tool

  • Answers "how much do I need to retire." Multiply yearly spending by 25. The tool does the math for you.
  • Yearly and monthly income. See what your savings could support.
  • Adjustable rate. Compare 3%, 3.5%, and 4% side by side.
  • A free retirement calculator for a first check. No spreadsheet needed.
  • Plain limits. Notes explain what the rule leaves out.

Who Can Use It

  • Pre-retirees. Check whether $800,000 could cover $32,000 a year.
  • Early retirees. Test lower rates, since a longer retirement needs more caution.
  • Current retirees. Review whether your withdrawals sit near the 4% line.
  • Young savers. Turn a spending goal into a savings target.

Tips for Best Results

  • Use real spending, not salary. Include housing, food, healthcare, and travel.
  • Subtract expected Social Security or pension income before setting a goal.
  • Remember taxes. Withdrawals from pre-tax accounts like a traditional 401(k) count as taxable income.
  • Test a range of rates. Ask what happens at 3.5%, not just 4%.
  • Pair it with a how long will my savings last calculator if you want year-by-year balances.
  • Recheck once a year, and review plans with a licensed planner.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the 4 percent rule?

You withdraw 4% of your savings in the first year of retirement. After that, you raise the dollar amount each year for inflation. It comes from 1990s research by planner William Bengen, using U.S. market history and a stock-and-bond mix.

How much do I need to retire with the 4% rule?

Multiply your yearly spending by 25. For $40,000 a year, you need about $1,000,000. Subtract Social Security or pension income first, since those cover part of your spending. Taxes and fees can raise the number.

How long will my money last with the 4 percent rule?

The rule aims for about 30 years, based on past U.S. data. It is not a promise. This is not a full how long will my money last calculator, because it doesn't model year-by-year returns. Poor early markets or longer retirements can shorten the timeline.

Does the 4 percent rule still work?

Many planners still use it as a starting point. Some suggest a lower rate for retirements longer than 30 years or for cautious plans. Nothing guarantees future market returns, so treat 4% as a guide.
