Puppy Weight Estimator: Predict Your Puppy's Adult Size

Last updated 2026-11-08 · By Faizan Khan Yousufzai

Wondering how big your puppy will get? Our free puppy weight estimator uses your puppy's age and current weight to predict a likely adult weight range. It helps new owners, adopters, and foster carers plan food, crates, and gear. Results are estimates, not guarantees, so treat them as a planning guide.

How the estimate works: This is an estimate based on a growth curve. A puppy reaches about half its adult weight at around 14 weeks (Toy and Small), 16 weeks (Medium), 20 weeks (Large) or 22 weeks (Giant). The adult weight is estimated as current weight x (age in weeks + that doubling age) / age in weeks, with months converted to weeks at 4.345 weeks per month and kilograms converted to pounds at 2.20462 lb per kg for the calculation. The result is shown as a range of the adult weight plus or minus 15%, rounded to 1 decimal, because growth varies by genetics, diet and health.

How to Use the Puppy Weight Estimator

  1. Weigh your puppy on a pet scale, or a baby scale for tiny pups.
  2. Enter your puppy's age in weeks or months.
  3. Enter their current weight.
  4. Choose a size group, such as toy, medium, or large.
  5. Click Estimate to see a likely adult weight range.

Why Use This Tool

  • Fast. Two or three inputs give you a result in seconds.
  • A range, not a guess. Each puppy weight estimate shows a spread, because puppies grow at different rates.
  • Works for mixed breeds. No breed standard? A size group still gives you a starting point.
  • Better planning. Pick the right crate, harness, and food budget before your puppy outgrows them.
  • Easy tracking. Re-run it to estimate puppy weight every few weeks and spot slow or fast growth.

Who Can Use It

  • New owners. Buy a crate and gear that fit an adult dog, not just a puppy.
  • Renters. Many leases cap pet weight, so check a likely size before you adopt.
  • Foster carers. Share a size estimate with adopters while pups are still small.
  • Breeders. Compare litter weights and notice pups that fall behind.

Tips for Best Results

  • Weigh at the same time of day, on the same scale, before a meal.
  • Use a recent weight. Estimating puppy adult weight gets more reliable as your puppy gets older.
  • Run the puppy weight estimator every two weeks and write down each result.
  • Ignore the paw size myth. Big paws don't reliably predict a big dog.
  • Don't feed extra to "hit" a number. Fast growth can strain joints in large breeds.
  • Ask your vet if weight gain stalls or jumps.

Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is a puppy weight estimate calculator?

It is a good guide, not a promise. Genetics, diet, and health all affect growth, and mixed breeds vary most. Treat the range as a planning tool and ask your vet to check your puppy's growth curve.

When do puppies stop growing?

Small breeds often finish by 9 to 12 months. Medium breeds usually take about a year. Large and giant breeds may keep filling out until 18 to 24 months. Your vet can tell you what to expect for your puppy's breed.

How can I estimate puppy adult weight for a mixed breed?

Choose the size group that fits best and enter age and weight. If you know a parent's adult weight, compare it with your result. A vet or a breed DNA test can narrow the range further.

Can paw size predict adult weight?

Not reliably. Many puppies have big paws they have yet to grow into, whatever the breed. Age, current weight, and size group give better clues. Even so, no method is exact, so recheck as your puppy grows.
